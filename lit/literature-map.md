# Literature map: risky-choice framing and the Asian Disease Problem

## Scope and search record

This map covers work needed to position the manuscript's two archival experiments. It is deliberately narrower than the full literature on framing: the target is risky-choice framing, especially the Asian Disease Problem (ADP), rather than attribute framing, goal framing, or generic gain/loss messaging.

The search was updated through August 18, 2026. It began with the original papers, Kühberger's 1998 meta-analysis and 2023 systematic review, then used backward and forward citation searches and targeted searches for the manuscript's manipulations: complete and incomplete options, complements, quantifiers, descriptor valence, response and outcome order, probability, task context, online samples, and individual differences. Recent synthesis and forward-search results were checked through publisher, journal, PubMed, institutional-repository, or Crossref records. The DOI is the record key wherever one exists.

This is a scoped evidence map, not a PRISMA systematic review. It is meant to make the manuscript's citation logic auditable and to prevent a long but unfocused bibliography. Studies of medical risk communication, political framing, foreign-language effects, neuroscience, development, and nonhuman choice were screened but retained only when they bear directly on an ADP claim made in the manuscript.

## What the literature establishes

1. The average directional ADP effect is highly reproducible. Meta-analyses, a multilaboratory replication, a 49-country study, and metastudies all find a shift toward the certain option in gain frames relative to loss frames.
2. Its magnitude is not a task-invariant constant. Domain, option completeness, complement structure, probabilities, context, and presentation procedures can alter the estimate.
3. Prospect theory is an important descriptive account, but the ADP alone does not identify its psychological mechanism. Fuzzy-trace, causal-schema, linguistic/pragmatic, explicated-valence, information-leakage, query-theory, and reason-based accounts make overlapping predictions for common task variants.
4. Missing-information explanations are incomplete. Clarifying quantifiers and complements can attenuate some versions, but effects survive intensive disambiguation and, in recent work, matched and complete descriptions.
5. Stable person-level susceptibility remains difficult to establish. Numeracy and reflection findings are mixed, and broad cognitive-style batteries have not consistently predicted frame-consistent choice.

## Boundaries and unresolved points

- The matrix does not treat the 2025 broad meta-analysis as an ADP-specific pooled estimate; its 40 studies cover multiple risky-decision domains.
- The 199/201 variants move the certain option one life below or above the gamble's expected outcome. The gain and loss descriptions remain objectively equivalent within each value, so each paired contrast still tests description invariance. Three nearby binary-choice points trace only a local choice profile; they do not identify a full value function or certainty equivalent in either frame.
- Response order and within-option outcome order have relatively thin direct literatures. Peng et al. (2017) is the closest direct comparator; the archival experiment adds useful precision but remains retrospective.
- The open-ended comments were collected after choice and coded under an overlapping, incompletely adjudicated scheme. They are not interchangeable with preregistered concurrent thought-listing or explicit moral-reason measures.
- A target-journal search should be rerun immediately before submission to catch online-first corrections and work published after August 18, 2026.

## Citation decisions for the manuscript

The manuscript should cite syntheses for broad claims and primary experiments for specific task features. It should not cite every row above. The core citation set is:

- robustness and heterogeneity: Kühberger (1998, 2023), Klein et al. (2014), Im and Chen (2022), DeKay et al. (2022), and Ding et al. (2025);
- competing accounts: Reyna and Brainerd (1991), Mandel (2001), Sher and McKenzie (2006), Tombu and Mandel (2015), and Wall et al. (2020);
- complement and wording evidence: Kühberger and Tanner (2010), Mandel (2014), Chick et al. (2016), Peng et al. (2017), Zhou et al. (2021), DeKay and Dou (2024), and DeKay (2026);
- individual differences: Stanovich and West (1998), LeBoeuf and Shafir (2003), Peters and Levin (2008), and Mandel and Kapler (2018).
